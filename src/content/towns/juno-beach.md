Juno Beach is a Palm Beach County town on the Atlantic, and its beach is a sea turtle nesting beach. That fact shapes building here in a way it does not in Jupiter's river neighborhoods or farther inland.

## Sea turtle lighting

Palm Beach County has regulated beachfront lighting to protect nesting sea turtles since 1987, and Juno Beach applies the county's sea turtle protection rules. The Town's permit application states that approval is required for new building construction within the protection zone, including new glass doors and windows and any new artificial lighting. A building permit and a separate sea turtle lighting permit are both submitted. Nesting runs from March through October, and the design of glass, fixtures and landscape lighting is planned with this in mind from the first drawings, rather than adjusted at the end.

## The coastal construction control line

Along the state's sandy beaches, Florida's Department of Environmental Protection sets a coastal construction control line. Work seaward of it needs a state permit unless it is exempt, and the structure must meet special siting and design criteria. Whether an oceanfront parcel is seaward of the line is among the first questions we ask.

## Wind and salt

An oceanfront site is exposed to wind and salt air. The Florida Building Code's wind provisions, including protection for windows and doors in wind-borne debris regions, and a careful choice of exterior materials, matter more here than they do inland.

## Permits

Juno Beach is a Town with its own building permit process. We confirm what is required with the Town before submitting.
